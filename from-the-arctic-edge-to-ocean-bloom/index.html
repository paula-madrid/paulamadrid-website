From the Arctic Edge to Ocean Bloom

  • Editorial Design
  • 3D Illustration

Year: 2022
While at: BIG - Bjarke Ingels Group

For this project, I developed the editorial concept and layout for the scientific publication documenting the Arctic Edge to Ocean Bloom ocean experiment. The book combines research-driven content with accessible visual storytelling to communicate the goals, methods, and significance of the expedition.

I was responsible for the full editorial design, including typography, layout, and visual hierarchy, as well as the creation of custom 3D illustrations to explain complex experimental setups and oceanographic concepts. These visuals were designed to bridge the gap between scientific data and public understanding, making the material more engaging and easier to grasp.

The result is a cohesive, visually compelling publication that supports the scientific narrative while standing as a strong piece of design communication in its own.

This map shows chlorophyll per m3 of seawater. Chlorophyll is a product of photosynthetic activity and is therefore used as a way to measure the primary production (of plankton and algae) in the oceans. The blue areas of the oceans indicate low marine primary productivity. We focused on analysing why these three locations show extremely high levels of chlorophyll.

I created this 3D infographic to illustrate the experimental setup for one of the marine research project exploring the impact of glacial rock flour on ocean ecosystems. The visual breaks down how buoys, mesocosm tubes, and sensors were arranged to collect data on physical, chemical, and biological changes in the North Atlantic over several weeks.
