## formgiving-book | Formgiving Book | Editorial Design
THUMB 5726591/a2b56172-bac4-41d0-ba49-555d3cdde76d.png?cX=47&cY=395&cW=1642&cH=1179
M Year: 2020
M While at: BIG - Bjarke Ingels Group
P As the Lead Graphic Designer at Bjarke Ingels Group (BIG), I led the team that developed this catalogue, which accompanied the <a href="../formgiving-exhibition/">exhibition of the same name</a> at the Danish Architecture Center in Copenhagen.
P Formgiving is a visionary attempt to look at the horizon of time. The Danish word for “design” is “formgivning,” which literally means to give form to that which has not yet taken shape. In other words, to give form to the future. Using our power to give form, rather than allowing the future to take shape, is more important now than ever, as humankind’s impact on the planet continues to increase and pose ever greater challenges to all life forms.
P Team: Paula Madrid, Geetika Bhutani, Ipek Akin, Jesslyn Guntur, Morgan Day
I 193 232 638 360 5497052/2d07107f-4c01-4d85-8d63-e90c4db46a8b.jpg
P The book is presented in a timeline, stretching from the Big Bang into the most distant future. Projects are structured around six strands of evolution: “Making,” “Sensing,” “Sustaining,” “Thinking,” “Healing,” and “Moving” the multimedia-based, interdisciplinary concepts encompassing the building industry.

I 628 800 203 200 5497052/32091520-ae50-495b-9c89-fee3b2ae8077.png
P It introduces the concept of the “10 Gifts” -- design elements that go beyond solving a problem to actively enhance the human experience. These gifts, such as sustainability, biodiversity, play, and social interaction, are intentionally embedded in projects to offer added value for both users and the wider community. Rather than designing just for function, BIG sees architecture as an opportunity to give something back -- to make buildings that are generous, engaging, and future-ready.
I 193 1088 638 360 5497052/fc56c0bb-b9d9-4809-8968-3c748595c906.png
P Formgiving is the third and final installment in Bjarke Ingels Group’s trilogy of architectural monographs with TASCHEN, following <i>Yes is More</i> (2009) and <i>Hot to Cold</i> (2015). Together, these books chart the evolution of BIG’s design philosophy and practice.

P The progression from ancient symbols to modern typefaces on the cover encapsulates the book's journey through time, from the Big Bang to speculative futures, reinforcing the idea that architecture and design are continuous processes of transformation and innovation.

P This infographic visually explores the Munsell Color System, invented by American artist and educator Albert H. Munsell in the early 20th century. The system classifies colors based on three key attributes: hue, value, and chroma. Modeled using Rhinoceros 3D software and further refined with Adobe Illustrator and InDesign, this project translates the complexities of color theory into a clear, engaging visual format. It offers an insightful guide to understanding color relationships and their practical applications in art, design, and science.

P I designed this set of eight postage stamps featuring homing pigeons—<i>palomas mensajeras</i> in Spanish. Known for their incredible ability to find their way home, these birds were once vital carriers of written messages.
